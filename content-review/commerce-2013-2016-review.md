# Commerce 2013–2016: source and answer review

Four supplied papers, 50 original questions each. This adds 200 questions to the published 2001–2012 bank. Original printed keys, wording and option counts are preserved, including missing-option defects. No official JAMB marking scheme was established.

New-paper academic classifications: {'corrected': 14, 'multiple': 13, 'noanswer': 3, 'misprint': 13, 'debatable': 24}; plus 4 preserved administrative booklet-identification instructions. All questions have a prepared offline Teach Me and a Key Points mapping. The combined bank has 54 lessons and 115 reviewed repeat groups (286 occurrences). These lessons cover the imported topics; this is not a claim of a completed full-syllabus audit.

## Source safeguards

- The four booklet-identification questions (2013–2016 Q1) are preserved as administrative instructions. They have no universal answer, credit any attempt, and are excluded from defective-question study counts and fast games. Their asterisks are not invented answer keys.
- Commerce 2005 Q39 and Commerce 2007 Q26 have only three printed options. No fourth option was invented.
- The 2005 Q39 compiled key says C/D, but no D is supplied. C is accepted and the source-key defect is explained.
- Source typos and conflicting figures are retained and explained; historical institutional/legal references are taught in context.
- Corrected answers enter Wrong Answers in Circulation; genuine multiple and missing-correct-option cases enter the corresponding Tricky Questions category. Only real distractor errors become Exam Traps. Repeated tested points retain their separate year occurrences.
- Fast games exclude multiple/no-correct-option questions. Defective-option questions credit any attempt without painting an offered answer factually correct.
- Published CRS metadata and content remain preserved; no shared backend configuration changes. The 45-second content download timeout remains.

### commerce-2013-q2

The type of labour that makes use of physical effort in production processes is the

A. unskilled labour
B. skilled labour
C. blue-collar job
D. white-collar job

Printed key: **A**. Treatment: **corrected**. Accepted: **C**.

C, blue-collar job, identifies work relying on physical or manual effort. Skill classifies training and expertise, not whether muscles are used. Skilled trades can be physical, so the source A, unskilled labour, does not follow from physical effort alone.
