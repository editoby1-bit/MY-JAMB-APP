# Commerce 2005–2008: source and answer review

Four supplied papers, 50 original questions each. This adds 200 questions to the published 2001–2004 bank. Original printed keys, wording and option counts are preserved, including missing-option defects. No official JAMB marking scheme was established.

New-paper classifications: {'corrected': 18, 'multiple': 15, 'noanswer': 4, 'misprint': 11, 'debatable': 8}. All questions have a prepared offline Teach Me and a Key Points mapping. The combined bank has 33 lessons and 22 reviewed repeat groups (49 occurrences). These lessons cover the imported topics; this is not a claim of a completed full-syllabus audit.

## Source safeguards

- Commerce 2005 Q39 and Commerce 2007 Q26 have only three printed options. No fourth option was invented.
- The 2005 Q39 compiled key says C/D, but no D is supplied. C is accepted and the source-key defect is explained.
- Source typos and conflicting figures are retained and explained; historical institutional/legal references are taught in context.
- Corrected answers enter Wrong Answers in Circulation; genuine multiple and missing-correct-option cases enter the corresponding Tricky Questions category. Only real distractor errors become Exam Traps. Repeated tested points retain their separate year occurrences.
- Fast games exclude multiple/no-correct-option questions. Defective-option questions credit any attempt without painting an offered answer factually correct.
- Published CRS metadata and content remain preserved; no shared backend configuration changes. The 45-second content download timeout remains.

## Reviewed answer and wording cases

### commerce-2005-q7

The most dangerous pollution is

A. water pollution
B. noise pollution
C. soil pollution
D. air pollution

Printed key: **D**. Treatment: **debatable**. Accepted: **D**.

D, air pollution, is the supplied classroom answer. Polluted air can expose large populations through breathing. However, there is no universal scientific ranking making it more dangerous than every water, soil or noise hazard.

### commerce-2005-q9

The compensation the insured gets depends heavily on the

A. economic situation
B. premium paid
C. risk suffered
D. insurer's buoyancy.

Printed key: **B**. Treatment: **corrected**. Accepted: **C**.

For an indemnity claim, eligible loss suffered is the direct basis of compensation, subject to policy limits and conditions. Premium paid purchases cover; it is not itself the amount reimbursed. Correct printed B to C.

- [Marine Insurance Act 1906, section 67: measure of indemnity](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/6/41/section/67)

### commerce-2005-q10

The computer that functions by taking discrete numbers and performing mathematical calculations is called

A. mainframe
B. digital
C. hybrid
D. analogue

Printed key: **C**. Treatment: **corrected**. Accepted: **B**.

A digital computer works with discrete numerical representations. Analogue computers model continuous quantities; hybrid systems combine analogue and digital features. Correct printed C to B.

### commerce-2005-q15

The best means of sending unwritten messages simultaneously to many branches of an organisation within a country is the

A. television
B. telephone
C. telegraph
D. radio

Printed key: **C**. Treatment: **corrected**. Accepted: **D**.

Radio can transmit spoken, unwritten messages to multiple receiving branches simultaneously. A telegraph transmits coded text; it does not best satisfy “unwritten.” Correct printed C to D for the stated broadcast setting.

### commerce-2005-q50

Given:
Sales 15,000
Opening stock 5,600
Purchases 9,700
Closing stock 4,400
Gross profit 4,500
Net profit 2,000
From the data above, calculate the rate of turnover.

A. 3.50 times
B. 3.000 times
C. 2.00 times
D. 2.18 times

Printed key: **D**. Treatment: **misprint**. Accepted: **D**.

Cost of goods sold = 5,600 + 9,700 − 4,400 = 10,900. Average stock = (5,600 + 4,400)/2 = 5,000. Stock turnover = 10,900/5,000 = 2.18 times, D. The supplied gross profit of 4,500 conflicts with these stock figures.
